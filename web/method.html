What the latency numbers mean

The deployment is US-hosted. A browser in Gujarat sits ~250ms of Pacific round-trip away before any work happens — so "answer visible in under 200ms" measured from an Indian laptop is impossible for any system. A system claiming it is measuring localhost, or lying.

Three numbers are published. None is hidden inside another.

NumberMeasuresSource
server_side_msfull request handling, network excluded — the headline SLOserver's monotonic clock, X-Server-Time-Ms
pipeline_msretrieval-to-answer stages, framework overhead excludedRequestTimer spans in the response body
client_observed_mswhat a caller at that location actually waitedbenchmark harness wall clock

To keep the headline honest, the benchmark also runs from a US GitHub Actions runner, co-located with the deployment — real deployed system, real HTTP, just not across an ocean. The India→US distribution is published separately with the network delta as its own series.

Why the answer arrives in two tiers

Measured time-to-first-token from the generation providers:

ProviderModelTTFT
Groqllama-3.3-70b-versatile477 ms
Groqopenai/gpt-oss-20b562 ms
Groqllama-3.1-8b-instant1573 ms
Cerebras—HTTP 402

Every one is multiples of the entire 200ms budget. Retrieve-then-generate cannot meet this target regardless of retrieval speed — that is arithmetic, not tuning. So Tier 1 is extractive: answer sentences copied verbatim from retrieved passages, grounded by construction, single-digit milliseconds. Tier 2 streams in afterwards and is discarded if it fails the grounding check.

Chunking Lab — measured, not asserted

Six strategies scored with real IR metrics against the dataset's is_selected relevance labels. Sampling is by query, so every sampled query's relevant passages are guaranteed present; chunks are collapsed to parent passages before scoring so a sentence-level index and a passage-level one are compared on the same footing.
